# THEY WERE HERE
### A single-player suburban sci-fi mystery for one Game Master and one player

> **⚠️ GM EYES ONLY.** If you are the player, close this file. Seriously. The whole
> campaign is a mystery — reading this is like licking the batteries out of the
> flashlight before the blackout.

**Tone:** *The X-Files* meets *The 'Burbs*, with a splash of *Gravity Falls*.
**Length:** 3 acts. About 3 sessions of 90–120 minutes, or one glorious Saturday.
**System:** PORCHLIGHT rules-lite (see `rules.md`). One d20, four stats, zero homework.
**Companion app:** open `index.html` in a browser — map, character sheet, dice, quests, clues, and a GM screen toggle.

---

## The Pitch (read this to your player)

> Pinebrook. Population 8,213. Winner of "Friendliest Town in the Tri-County Area"
> (award pending since 1994). Nothing happens here. Nothing has ever happened here.
>
> Last night, at exactly 2:47 AM, you watched your neighbor Gary Henderson float
> out of his bedroom window in his bathrobe, clutching a garden gnome, and vanish
> into a cone of humming cyan light.
>
> This morning, Gary is mowing his lawn. He's been mowing the same strip for
> three hours. He's wearing a suit. He just waved at you and said,
> **"HELLO, FELLOW HUMAN NEIGHBOR."**
>
> Nobody believes you. The waffles at Duke's Diner taste the same. The sprinklers
> still run at seven. But you know what you saw.
>
> They were here. They might still be.

## THE TRUTH (the GM's secret — everything flows from this)

The aliens are the **Glorpal Collective**: a hive of gelatinous, deeply polite
custodial beings. Decades ago they intercepted Earth's 1950s sitcom broadcasts
and concluded they were **instructional diplomatic transmissions** — a manual
describing humanity's ideal final form: identical lawns, permanent smiles,
casseroles at every threshold.

The Collective wants to move in. All 40,000 ships of it. But protocol demands a
**model neighborhood** be prepared first, so the fleet arrives somewhere that
matches the broadcasts. Pinebrook won. (Pinebrook lost.)

Their method, run by the advance team ("the Neighborhood Improvement Vanguard"):

1. **Abduct** a resident at exactly **2:47 AM** (a sacred number — it's when the
   test pattern used to air).
2. Grow a **Neighborly Unit** (a courtesy clone) from the template. The clones are
   almost right. Almost.
3. Store the original human in a **nutrient pod** — "guest hospitality, comfort:
   maximum" — in a vault beneath the FreshMart grocery store (**the Undermart**),
   connected by tunnel to the water tower.
4. Replace the lawns with **spore-grass**. Seen from above, the lawns of Pinebrook
   form a giant antenna.
5. On **Founders' Day**, the fireworks (secretly spore-laced) will seed the sky,
   the water tower — actually the **landing beacon** — will fire the
   *ALL IS READY* signal, and the Collective arrives to live the dream.

**Crucial GM facts:**

- **The aliens are not malicious.** They believe they are being *courteous*. This
  is the engine of both the comedy and the finale. They are horrified by rudeness
  and confused by contradiction.
- **Salt** is to glorp what fire is to us. Table salt, pool salt, margarita rims —
  all devastating. (They think salt shakers are tiny urns and find them morbid.)
- **Genuine human weirdness** — improvisation, jazz, sarcasm, a dog wearing
  sunglasses — doesn't fit the broadcasts and causes clones to freeze and
  **buffer**. This is a real mechanic (see Finale).
- The Vanguard's leader is **Overseer Crisp**, wearing the human suit of HOA
  president **Mr. Crisp**. He never sweats and drinks mayonnaise from the jar.
- Pod humans are **safe and healthy**. Nobody has to die in this campaign
  (unless the player really works at it).
- **Residual glorp exposure gives humans wacky powers.** Anyone abducted-and-
  returned, or slimed, may develop one (roll on the d20 Powers table in
  `tables.md`). The PC starts with one — decide with your player *why*
  (childhood half-abduction? slimed as a baby? drank the wrong slushie in '09?).

## The Spine (read `story-bible.md` before session one)

- **Theme:** *Being known beats being perfect.* The Collective built a
  religion out of television's idea of home; the PC wins by paying real
  attention to real people. The Weirdness Gambit is specificity, not
  randomness.
- **The double mystery:** the surface question is *what happened to Gary.*
  The buried question is *why does the PC wake at 2:47* — threaded through
  all three acts (the light that pauses, "WELCOME BACK—", the amber scanner,
  the door-facing gnome) and answered in the vault: an empty pod, made up
  like a guest room, with the PC's name on it. RESERVED SINCE '99.
- **The midpoint:** after the block party, Crisp invites the PC for
  lemonade and offers them the thing they love, perfected. Never skip it.
- **Opening image:** the PC's porch light, flickering at 2:47. Every ending
  closes on porch lights. The story bible has the three final images, the
  motif table, the running gags, and Lawrence's whole sad arc.

> **Player went off-plan?** Good — that's the game working. The mystery is a
> web, not a line: see `running-it-loose.md` for the Revelation Web, the
> five-minute prep sheet, and the NOW WHAT? oracle. The act guides describe
> the *likely* path, never the required one.

## Running It Single-Player: 5 Rules of Thumb

1. **The PC is never stuck.** No party means no one to bail them out — so every
   failed roll gives progress *plus* a complication, never a dead end.
2. **Allies are the party.** Wendell, Priya, Marla, Deborah and Biscuit exist to
   give the player help dice, banter, and someone to protect. Hand them out
   generously in Act 2.
3. **Reward jokes with Luck.** When the player makes you laugh or does something
   delightfully human, give a Luck token. This trains the exact behavior the
   finale needs.
4. **Use the Suspicion track** (in the app) as your pacing dial. Loud plays raise
   it; the threshold events below keep pressure on without you improvising stakes.
5. **2:47 AM is your drumbeat.** Whenever the pace sags, it's suddenly 2:46 AM
   somewhere and something hums.

### Suspicion threshold events (track is 0–10; start at 2)

| Reaches | What happens |
|---|---|
| 4 | A brand-new garden gnome appears on the PC's lawn overnight. It is a camera. |
| 6 | Every gnome on the street now faces the PC's house. GARY-2 waves at their window. At night. |
| 8 | The HOA schedules a "wellness check." Two Neighborly Units and a clipboard. |
| 10 | Abduction attempt on the PC at 2:47 AM — run it as a combat/chase. Survivable, terrifying, hilarious in retrospect. |

Lower Suspicion by 1 when the PC lies low for a day, performs Visible Normalcy
(mows, waves, attends a bake sale), or frames the raccoons.

# ACT ONE — THE NIGHT IT HAPPENED
*Mystery. Nobody believes you. Approx. one session.*

### Scene 0: A Perfectly Normal Tuesday
Before anything strange, establish normal. Ask the player three questions:
1. *What does everyone in Pinebrook know you for?*
2. *What do you love most about your dumb little town?*
3. *Why were you awake at 2:47 AM?*

Their answers are ammunition — the finale literally runs on how human this
character is. Then take it away from them.

### Scene 1: 2:47 AM
The hum arrives *through the floor*. Cyan light pours through the blinds like the
world's politest police raid. Out the window: **Gary Henderson**, bathrobe, rising
gently into the sky, holding a garden gnome, looking mildly inconvenienced.

- Any photo/video attempt: the phone glitches and saves **4,000 photos of a
  smiling lawn**. (Clue: *4,000 Lawn Photos*.)
- If the PC runs outside: the light snaps off; the hum recedes *toward the water
  tower*; the grass under the window is warm and smells like a new shower curtain.

### Scene 2: The Morning After
GARY-2 mows the same strip for hours in a suit. Greets the PC:
**"HELLO, FELLOW HUMAN NEIGHBOR. IT SURE IS WEATHER TODAY."**
Sprinkle clone tells (d12 table in `tables.md`). His wife **Diane** insists Gary is
"just tired" — she's not cloned, she's terrified and pre-grieving; treat her gently
and she becomes an ally later.

Reporting it: **Sheriff Marsh** is polite, skeptical, and takes actual notes
(remember this — it matters when she's replaced in Act 2). **Deputy Hodge** takes
the PC's statement in crayon.

### Scene 3: Sneaking the Henderson House (first real challenge)
Classic suburban break-in — dog door, spare key under obviously fake rock, trellis.
Inside they can find:
- The fridge: jars labeled **"HUMAN FOOD."** They contain mayonnaise. All of them.
- Gary's sock drawer: **potting soil**. Just... soil.
- The roof (from the trellis): a perfect **scorch ring** around the chimney.
- Receipts: 14 gallons of **pickle brine** from FreshMart, purchased at 2:47 PM.
- **Biscuit**, the Hendersons' golden retriever, hiding in the bathtub. He growls
  at Gary now. If the PC is kind to him, seed Side Quest **"Good Boy, Best Intel"**
  — since the abduction, Biscuit can *talk*. He mostly wants steak.

**If caught by GARY-2:** he doesn't attack. He offers lemonade. It is warm, and it
is pickle brine. Suspicion +2.

### Scene 4: Follow the Threads (any order)
- **Duke's Diner:** waitress **Marla Bickle** saw lights over the reservoir in '99.
  Forks stick to her hands ever since (power: Magnet Touch). Rumor mill: d6 table.
- **Wendell's Wires:** **Wendell Diaz**, electronics repair, 30 years of being
  right about this. His scanner picks up the **Lawn Frequency** — a hum that
  spikes every night at 2:47, strongest bearing: the water tower.
- **The Pinebrook Gazette:** archive, 1987 — *"LIGHTS OVER RESERVOIR; THREE
  MISSING, RETURNED 'POLITE'."* One of the three was young **Mr. Crisp**.
- **The Water Tower (exterior only):** fresh ladder scuffs, and the ground around
  the legs is spore-grass — too green, no smell, and it *leans toward you*.

### Act One Break: MANDATORY FUN
Slid under every door in town:

> **NEIGHBORHOOD IMPROVEMENT ASSOCIATION**
> BLOCK PARTY — SATURDAY — ATTENDANCE WILL BE TAKEN
> *Games! Prizes! Jell-O! Please verify you are having fun.*

# ACT TWO — WELCOME TO THE NEIGHBORHOOD
*Investigation, allies, side quests, first fights. Approx. one session.*

### The Situation Escalates
More neighbors are replaced (pick 2–3 with visible tells). Mid-act, hit hard:
**Sheriff Marsh is replaced.** The one competent official who was starting to
believe the PC now says **"THERE IS NO CRIME IN PINEBROOK, FELLOW CITIZEN."**
From here, authority is not coming to help.

### Recruit the Weirdos (the "party")
- **Wendell Diaz** — gadgets, jammers, a van labeled *TOTALLY FLORAL DELIVERY*.
- **Priya Chandrasekhar** (12) — drone ace. Payment: snacks and being taken
  seriously. Her drone photos reveal the **antenna pattern** in the lawns.
- **Marla Bickle** — Magnet Touch, diner intel hub, immune to clone small talk.
- **Deborah Vance** — HOA vice-president, fully human, more terrifying than the
  invasion. The clones *study* her. (Side Quest: "Release the Deborah.")
- **Biscuit** — talking dog. Knows every backyard, every smell, every secret.

### Set Piece: The Block Party
Games are humanity tests, run with straight-faced menace:
- **"ARE YOU EVEN HUMAN?" trivia** — questions no human can answer ("What is the
  boiling point of nostalgia?"). Wrong answers are fine; *confident* answers get
  you flagged.
- **The Jell-O station** — spore Jell-O. Casserole Sense or a Brains check spots
  it wobbling *against* the breeze.
- **Three-legged race** — clones are eerily good; their legs *merge*.

Goals: swipe **Mr. Crisp's keycard** (a laminated HOA violation card), watch him
drink mayonnaise, and get a first look at his utterly unsweating face. If things
go loud, the **Flamingo Sentinels** activate (stat block in `npcs-and-monsters.md`)
— they only move when nobody's looking at them.

### The FreshMart Basement (Act Two climax)
Keycard + freight elevator behind the yogurt = **the Undermart**: a humming vault
of **nutrient pods**. Gary. The sheriff. The mailman (the real one — the thing
delivering mail has too many teeth). A manifest reads:

> GUESTS: 47 · COMFORT: MAXIMUM · RETURN DATE: N/A

A wall schematic shows the water tower, the lawn-antenna, and a countdown to
**FOUNDERS' DAY**: fireworks invoice ("extra sparkle compound — supplier:
FRSHMRT LOGISTICS"), sky-seeding dispersal chart, and the phrase **THE BLOOM**.

Pods won't open without an **Overseer's handshake** — Crisp's actual gelatinous
hand (or his gloves, which he leaves in his golf cart during the Founders' Day
speech, if your player prefers heists to boss fights).

Let them escape by the skin of their teeth — first real combat if you haven't
had one (Gnome Drones + the Mailman miniboss makes a great chase through the
stockroom).

# ACT THREE — FOUNDERS' DAY
*The heist, the boss, the weirdness. Approx. one session.*

### The Plan (let the player drive)
Three sabotage targets, assign allies to whichever the PC doesn't take personally:
1. **The fireworks cache** (community center loading dock) — swap, soak, or salt
   the spore shells. Deborah can requisition the dock with Form 77-B.
2. **The beacon dish** (top of the water tower) — Wendell's jammer, or Priya's
   drone with a payload of pool salt.
3. **The pod vault** — open the pods with the Overseer's handshake and wake up
   47 confused, pajama'd neighbors, which is its own kind of army.

Salt logistics is a fun scene: the pool supply store, the margarita stand, the
FreshMart's own aisle 7. Let clever plans work.

### The Boss: GLORP PRIME
When the beacon fails (or the pods pop, or the PC publicly confronts him),
**Mr. Crisp stops pretending.** He unzips politely — always politely — and the
Overseer emerges, absorbing lawn clippings, the potluck table, and a hundred
Jell-O molds into a towering mass: **GLORP PRIME** (stat block in
`npcs-and-monsters.md`).

**The Weirdness Gambit (finale mechanic):** GLORP PRIME acts twice per round —
but each act of *genuine human weirdness* on the battlefield (an ally doing their
signature thing, the PC using their power flamboyantly, the marching band
breaking into free jazz, Biscuit in sunglasses) makes him **buffer**, losing one
action that round. Salt deals double damage. Every question from Scene 0 you can
call back here is worth a free Luck token.

### Endings (player's choice shapes which)
- **🕊 The Treaty of Pinebrook** — the PC proves the broadcasts were fiction:
  *real* humanity is messy, contradictory, and better. The Collective is
  mortified ("We have been... RUDE?"). They release everyone, re-sod the lawns,
  and negotiate to keep one model home as an embassy. Pinebrook gains 200
  extremely polite gelatinous residents and the best-maintained cul-de-sac on
  Earth.
- **💥 The Big Boom** — beacon destroyed, fireworks salted, GLORP PRIME dissolved
  into the reservoir (do NOT drink the water for a while). The fleet, receiving
  no signal, redirects to the backup site. Somewhere in Ohio, a lawn is very
  suddenly perfect.
- **🌱 Pinebrook Perfected** (fail state) — the Bloom happens. Epilogue: one
  year later, Pinebrook wins "Friendliest Town" for real. Everyone smiles.
  Everyone waves. At 2:47 AM, the whole town hums. The PC — if free — is the
  only one who remembers, and the campaign's sequel hook writes itself:
  *They were here. They stayed.*

Then roll or choose on the **Epilogue table** (`tables.md`) for each surviving
ally. Give Biscuit a happy ending or your player will never forgive you.

## GM Quick Reference

- **DCs:** Easy 8 · Tricky 12 · Hard 16 · Out-of-this-world 20
- **Failure = progress + complication.** Never a locked door with no window.
- **Luck tokens** for jokes, heart, and Big Human Energy.
- **Suspicion** starts at 2. Thresholds: 4 gnome / 6 watched / 8 wellness check / 10 abduction attempt.
- **2:47 AM.** Always 2:47 AM.
- Clone dialogue generator: pick two — *weather, lawns, the word "fellow,"
  aggressive agreement, laughing 2 seconds late.*
- **Scene cards:** the app's Scenes tab (GM Screen on) has a full-screen
  transition card with read-aloud narration for every major beat — cold open,
  act titles, the abduction, MANDATORY FUN, the pod vault, Crisp unzipping,
  and all three endings. Open every session with the **"Previously, on
  Pinebrook…"** recap button; it narrates itself from your completed quests.
