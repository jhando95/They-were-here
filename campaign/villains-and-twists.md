# Villains & Twists — the Suspense Playbook
### Active opposition, the Gaslight Engine, the Twist Deck · ⚠️ GM EYES ONLY

The Vanguard doesn't just hide — **it hunts back.** This chapter turns the
mystery into a thriller: villains with moves of their own, a second clock that
makes the town doubt the PC's sanity, and ten prepared twists with fuses.

---

## 1. THE VISE (why the player always feels squeezed)

Two clocks, both in the app, pulling in opposite directions:

- **👁 NEIGHBORHOOD WATCH (Suspicion)** — how much the *aliens* have noticed the PC.
- **🫥 REPUTATION (Discredit)** — how much the *humans* think the PC is losing it.

Almost every bold move raises one of them. Investigate loudly → the Vanguard
notices. Lie low and stay quiet → MARSH-2's whisper campaign runs unopposed
(+1 Reputation per session she is active and unanswered). There is no safe
lane. That pressure — not any single fight — is the campaign's suspense.

### The Reputation track (0–10, starts at 1)

| Reaches | What happens |
|---|---|
| 3 | The Gazette runs a small item: *"LOCAL RESIDENT ASKS ODD QUESTIONS."* Arlene apologizes privately. ("MORTGAGE CITED. SHAME EXPRESSED.") |
| 5 | The allies get pressured: Priya's mom bans "the weird adult," Wendell's shop draws a surprise permit inspection, Marla's manager schedules her opposite the PC's usual hours. |
| 7 | **THE INTERVENTION** (set-piece, §4): the town gathers, with casseroles and gentle voices, to help the PC "get some rest." |
| 9 | **THE COMMITTAL**: paperwork for "voluntary rest at a nice facility upstate" is being processed. The van is scheduled. The van is very comfortable. |

**Reputation rises (+1):** public claims without hard proof · getting caught
somewhere weird · a failed public scene · a MARSH-2 press moment · **the
Tuesday Truthers publicly endorsing the PC** (the kiss of death — nothing
sinks credibility like Terrence agreeing with you, loudly, with binders).

**Reputation falls:** −1 show hard evidence *privately* to one person (they
become a quiet believer) · −1 a week of Visible Normalcy · −1 Deborah Vance
vouches ("I have reviewed this resident's documentation. It is IMPECCABLE.")
· −2 Arlene publishes verifiable proof (needs two Hard evidence pieces, §2).

**GM voice tip:** never say "you lose Reputation points." Say *"Diane looks at
you the way you looked at Gary."* The meter is for you; the dread is for them.

## 2. Evidence rules (proof is a game object)

Three tiers. The tier decides what a piece of proof can do:

| Tier | Examples | Power |
|---|---|---|
| **Rumor** (0) | testimony, Truther corkboard, "I saw it" | Recruits weirdos; moves no needles |
| **Trace** (1) | photos, spore-grass sample, brine receipts, gnome fragments | Privately convinces ONE person (−1 Reputation) |
| **Hard** (2) | The Witness camcorder footage, the pod manifest, the fireworks invoice, Crisp's DECLINED letter, a living freed pod-guest | Convinces anyone shown privately; **two** Hard pieces = Arlene front page (−2 Reputation, town wakes up) |

- Going public with less than two Hard pieces **backfires**: the town's denial
  reflex kicks in, +1 Reputation.
- Evidence left at home, logged with the station, or mailed **gets confiscated**
  (MARSH-2, §3) or **lost in the mail** (THE MAILMAN — "delivery attempted").
  The Truth Board in the app is the player's evidence inventory — make them
  think about where the *physical* copies live.

## 3. The Opposition (villains who make moves)

Run one Opposition move per session minimum. They are polite. They are patient.
They are *working*.

### 🚔 SHERIFF MARSH-2 — the Gaslighter-in-Chief

The Vanguard's masterstroke: replace the one official who was starting to
believe the PC, and aim her at the *investigation* instead of the aliens.
Her genius is that she never calls the PC a liar — she's **concerned**.

**Stat block:** HP 12 · Dodge 12 · +3 baton (1d6) or taser (1d4 + lose your
next action). **"I'M CONCERNED" aura:** while MARSH-2 is present and speaking,
the PC has disadvantage on Charm with bystanders. **CONFISCATION:** if the PC
is ever detained, she takes one piece of evidence ("THIS IS EVIDENCE NOW,
FELLOW CITIZEN. EVIDENCE OF NOTHING.").

**Her escalation ladder (one rung per act beat):**
1. A friendly porch visit: "People are WORRIED about you." (+1 Reputation)
2. A town-hall minute: "a resident spreading unrest" — no name. Everyone knows.
3. Pressure on allies (the Reputation-5 events — she's the author of all of them).
4. The Intervention (§4), which she organizes but attends only as "support."
5. The Committal paperwork. She signs it with a pen that never runs out.

**Her tells (plant early, pay off late):**
- Real Marsh drank coffee black with three sugars. MARSH-2 orders
  **"COFFEE. TEMPERATURE: COFFEE."**
- She's stopped doing the secret handshake she and Hodge invented in 2011.
  She doesn't know it exists. **Hodge noticed on day one** (Twist 10).
- Her incident reports are suddenly typo-free, filed in perfect fours.

**Her weaknesses:** procedure (a citizen's-arrest form filed by Chuck's
laminated card, or Deborah citing Section 9 detainment rules, freezes her for
a scene — she cannot be impolite OR improper) · Hodge's rituals · and the real
Marsh's hidden case notes (Twist 10), which prove the last thing she did as a
human was start to believe the PC.

### 📬 THE MAILMAN — the Hunter

Not just a miniboss: the Vanguard's search pattern. Ernestine Cobb has it
right — *"nobody walks a route like that."* He sweeps for un-replaced
households buying salt, intercepts evidence in the post, and leaves
"MISSED DELIVERY" slips at homes scheduled for 2:47 pickups. When the PC gets
close to a secret, the next clue arrives *pre-opened, re-taped, with a
courtesy note: "DAMAGED IN TRANSIT. WE APOLOGIZE."*

### 💇 BEV-2 — Counterintelligence with a Blowout

Everything said in Beverly's chair reaches Crisp within 24 hours — and
everything the *Vanguard* says reaches her chair. The PC's leak-hunting scene:
feed three different suspects three different fake secrets, see which one
comes back with The Pinebrook haircut attached. (Remember: she MUST reciprocate
gossip. The pipe runs both ways. She is their best asset and their worst hole.)

### 🕴 CRISP — the Planner

Crisp never rages; he **budgets**. Each PC victory triggers the next line item:

| The PC... | Crisp's countermeasure |
|---|---|
| Gets the Gazette interested | FreshMart doubles its back-page ad buy; Arlene's mortgage gets "refinanced, as a courtesy" |
| Frees a pod guest early | Pod hall goes to "PREMIUM COMFORT" — biometric recesses (now it MUST be his hand, not just the glove) |
| Recruits Deborah | The Vanguard proposes *her* for HOA President. A promotion. A muzzle. She almost takes it. Let her waver — real stakes |
| Kills the block party | Founders' Day security detail: MARSH-2 + four Units + a "STRUCTURALLY NECESSARY" fence around the tower |
| Publicly humiliates a clone | Nothing. He sends a thank-you card for the feedback. This is somehow the scariest one |

## 4. Challenge set-pieces (drop in when a session needs teeth)

- **THE FRIENDLY INTERROGATION.** MARSH-2 brings the PC in — "just a chat."
  Social combat: three exchanges; each exchange the PC picks *truth, lie, or
  silence* and rolls (Charm to sell it, Brains to spot her trap question,
  Weird to notice she doesn't blink between exchanges). Two failures = she
  keeps a piece of evidence and +1 Reputation. Two successes = her mask slips
  once — a three-second buffer, on camera, if anyone thought to film it (Hard
  evidence!). Hodge, in the corner, drawing the whole time.
- **THE INTERVENTION.** Reputation 7. The living room. Diane, the pastor, two
  neighbors, a circle of chairs, seventeen casseroles. No dice at first — make
  the player *talk their way through love*. The out: spot the one attendee
  who's a Unit (planted to steer the meeting), expose it with a clone test IN
  FRONT of everyone (rounds-singing, bad joke + two-Mississippi, the pretzel)
  — the intervention flips into the town's first mass awakening. Fail: 
  "voluntary rest" gains momentum (+2 Reputation) — and the twist: the pastor
  slips the PC a note on the way out: *"I froze eleven of them with a hymn.
  Come see me."* Even failure recruits.
- **THE EVIDENCE HEIST.** MARSH-2 confiscated the goods; the station lockup
  has them, filed under NOTHING, box 2:47. Hodge leaves the window unlatched
  every Tuesday ("for the moths"). In and out during her patrol loop —
  8 minutes, 3 obstacles, and the real Marsh's coffee mug is still on her
  desk, washed and turned upside down, which MARSH-2 does every single
  morning without knowing why. Let the player notice that. Let it hurt.
- **THE TAIL.** The cruiser follows at exactly 2 mph below the limit for
  days. Losing it: any stat, DC 12, *new method every time* — repeats
  auto-fail. Biscuit's backyard network trivializes one escape per session,
  at the price of narrating the smells.

## 5. THE TWIST DECK (ten fuses — also in the app's Scenes tab)

Each twist has a **Plant** (seed it early, casually), a **Fire** (the reveal
moment), and **Fallout**. Track them in the app: *idle → armed (planted) →
fired.* Fire one per session, tops. Never fire one you didn't plant.

1. **THE GNOME INDOORS.** *Plant:* the lawn gnome at Suspicion 4. *Fire:* days
   later it's gone from the lawn — and standing in the PC's hallway, facing
   the bedroom door. Nobody moved it. *Fallout:* pure dread; the house is no
   longer neutral ground. (Early-campaign jolt; costs nothing, buys terror.)
2. **ONE OF US.** *Plant:* three retroactive-visible tells across two sessions
   on ONE ally (never Biscuit, never Hodge): the wrong coffee order, a
   too-clean laugh, a first-ever use of the PC's full legal name. *Fire:* the
   PC catches them watering plastic flowers. *Fallout:* the ally's real self is
   in the Undermart; the Unit has been feeding plans to Crisp for two sessions
   — recount every scene it attended. The player will do this instantly and
   audibly. Let them.
3. **THE TEMPLATE.** *Plant:* the PC's Session-Zero answer about why they wake
   at 2:47; Marla's "nobody believed me either." *Fire:* in the real Marsh's
   case file or the Undermart records: the PC's own childhood file. Taken in
   '99. RETURNED: POLITE. **NOTE: TEMPLATE RETAINED.** Their power is
   residue; the Collective has had a copy of THEM for decades. *Fallout:*
   somewhere in the vault is a pod-fresh PC-2, unactivated. Whether it ever
   activates is your call — even the threat changes how they sleep.
4. **THE NINTH GNOME.** *Plant:* Priya's count is always off by one; chalk
   arrows appear near danger. *Fire:* one gnome is a defector — a scout who
   read the broadcasts AND the town, and chose the town. It's been warning
   people for weeks. Nobody reads gnome. *Fallout:* a tiny, silent, ceramic
   ally with total surveillance access. (Pairs with the Gnorman artifact.)
5. **HERB'S CHOICE.** *Plant:* Deborah's husband "replaced in March"; her
   written apology. *Fire:* Herb's pod, opened — and Herb asks for *five more
   minutes.* The pods are the first vacation he's had in 31 years. *Fallout:*
   the rescue is suddenly morally itchy; Deborah's face does something
   nobody's ever seen it do. Comedy and heartbreak in one beat.
6. **HE ASKED TO GO.** *Plant:* the Gazette's spiked 1987 follow-up; Coral's
   14-year microfiche checkout. *Fire:* teenage Crisp's interview: he
   *volunteered.* The loneliest kid at the reservoir asked the light to take
   him, and the suit isn't a prison — it's a boy who never wanted to come
   back. *Fallout:* the Treaty path opens; "GLORP PRIME" becomes a person you
   can talk to mid-fight. The finale's Charm DC drops if the PC uses his name.
   His first name is Lawrence. Nobody has used it since 1987.
7. **THEY'RE ALREADY HERE.** *Plant:* Doug Pramuk's telescope "primer flash"
   log; the reservoir's wrong-way shadow. *Fire:* the fleet isn't coming —
   it *arrived in 1987* and is sleeping under the reservoir. The tower doesn't
   summon; it *wakes.* *Fallout:* optional escalation dial for Act Three —
   the Big Boom ending now has a countdown swim. Use only if your player
   thrives under pressure.
8. **THE DEFECTOR.** *Plant:* TODD-2's hibachi, the soundless high-fives.
   *Fire:* TODD-2 flips — first ally from the other side — and leaks one page
   of the schedule: **the PC's own abduction date. It's this Friday.**
   *Fallout:* a countdown on everything; sleeping becomes a tactical decision.
   ("GANG. GANG. I SHOULDN'T BE TELLING YOU THIS, GANG.")
9. **THE BUNDT CAKE.** *Plant:* the Reputation-7 wellness check's gift.
   *Fire:* Casserole Sense or a lab-minded ally finds the pod-mint baked
   inside. They weren't checking on the PC. **They were pre-boarding them.**
   *Fallout:* the "wellness" apparatus is revealed as the intake pipeline —
   and the PC now holds a Hard evidence pastry.
10. **HODGE KNEW.** *Plant:* every crayon drawing that was too accurate; the
    unlatched Tuesday window; "template incoherent." *Fire:* the crayon box.
    Inside: the real Marsh's case notes (she believed the PC — it's in
    writing), the handshake ledger ("SHE FORGOT OUR SHAKE. MARSH WOULD NOT
    FORGET. I AM PRETENDING NOT TO KNOW."), and a drawing of the PC labeled
    "THE ONE WHO'LL FIX IT." *Fallout:* Hodge was never dim — he was *waiting*
    for someone safe to hand this to. The station is now a safehouse on
    Tuesdays, and the saddest sweetest scene in the campaign is his to play.

## 6. Pacing the dread (cheat card)

- **Every session:** one Opposition move + at most one fired twist.
- **Every act:** MARSH-2 climbs one ladder rung; Crisp pays for one countermeasure.
- **The vise rule:** when the player is comfortable, raise whichever clock is
  lower. When they're paranoid about the RIGHT things, reward them — a clue,
  a defector, a quiet believer.
- **The 2:47 rule stands:** when in doubt, it is suddenly 2:46 AM, and
  somewhere on the street, a porch light comes on by itself.
