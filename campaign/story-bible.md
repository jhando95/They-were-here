# The Story Bible — the Editor's Cut
### Theme, the double mystery, motifs, running gags, and Lawrence · ⚠️ GM EYES ONLY

This is the depth layer: what the campaign is *about*, and the craft tools —
echoes, runners, voice notes — that make the mystery feel inevitable and the
comedy land like heart. Nothing here adds prep; it changes how you play what
you already have.

---

## 1. The theme (say it to yourself, never to the player)

> **Being known beats being perfect.**

The Collective watched forty years of broadcasts about perfect neighbors and
never once saw a person get *known*. That's the whole tragedy: they built a
religion out of television's idea of home. It's also the whole comedy — a
species doing hospitality with the manual upside down. And it's the win
condition: the Weirdness Gambit isn't randomness, it's **specificity**. Biscuit
in sunglasses buffers GLORP PRIME because only someone who truly knows that
dog would know he'd love it. When the player does something in the finale,
ask yourself: *is this generic wacky, or is this a thing only THIS person
would do for THIS town?* Reward the second twice as hard.

Every ally is on-theme: Wendell (right for 30 years, never believed), Priya
(never taken seriously), Marla (never believed), Deborah (feared, never
known), Hodge (underestimated his whole life), Duke (never even *seen* — he's
been in the kitchen 11 years). The PC saves Pinebrook by doing the thing the
aliens can't: paying real attention to real people. The census isn't a joke
list. It's the arsenal.

**Opening image** (use it): the PC's own porch light, flickering at 2:47 AM,
the night everything starts. Porch lights are the campaign's heartbeat — see
the motif table. Each ending closes on them.

## 2. The double mystery (Thread B — the PC's own 2:47)

The surface mystery is Gary. The buried mystery is the player character —
**why do they wake at 2:47?** Twist 3 (The Template) answers it, but a file
discovery only devastates if the question has been breathing on the player's
neck for three acts. Thread the needle:

**Act One (two beats, both deniable):**
- During the abduction, Weird DC 8 to notice: the light *paused over the PC's
  house first.* A breath. Like it was checking an address. Do not explain.
- GARY-2's first-ever greeting, before the famous one: **"WELCOME BACK—"**
  three-second buffer — "—HELLO, FELLOW HUMAN NEIGHBOR." If questioned, he has
  no memory of saying it. He doesn't. The *network* said it.

**Act Two (the pattern emerges):**
- Clones keep half-verifying the PC: the block party trivia scanner blinks
  amber, not red — "RETURNING... GUEST?" — before a Unit apologetically
  overrides it.
- The gnome on the PC's lawn faces **the front door**, not the street. Priya,
  checking her charts, quietly: "They only do that at houses they've already
  done." Let that sit.
- Marla, one night, wiping the counter, not looking up: "It paused for you,
  didn't it. Over your house." Beat. "It never paused for me."
- And the midpoint: **Crisp's Offer** (§3), where the villain says the quiet
  part: *"YOU ARE NOT LIKE THE OTHER RESIDENTS. YOU WATCH."*

**Act Three (the payoff, in the vault):** when the pods open — or earlier, if
they explore the Undermart's back row — one pod stands apart. Empty. Made up
like a guest room: folded blanket, a mint on the pillow, and a nameplate older
than all the others, the tape gone yellow. **The PC's name. RESERVED SINCE
'99.** They kept the room ready. They always believed the PC would come back.
(Run it silent. No sting. Let the player read the nameplate out loud
themselves — they will.)

This recontextualizes everything: the power, the 2:47 waking, the Collective's
odd courtesy toward them — the PC isn't the random witness. **They're the one
that got away, and the invasion has been quietly leaving the porch light on
for them ever since.** In the Treaty ending, this is why negotiation is even
possible: to the Collective, the PC is family who chose the other side, which
means the other side must have something worth choosing.

## 3. The missing midpoint — THE OFFER (new Act Two scene, run it after the block party)

Every good mystery pauses in the middle so the villain can offer the hero a
door marked EXIT. 🎬 The app has a scene card for it: **Lemonade with Mr.
Crisp.**

> The invitation is handwritten. Real handwriting — you can tell somebody
> practiced. Two wicker chairs on a porch that has never once been sat on.
> A pitcher of lemonade, beading in the heat. Real lemonade — he researched
> it. He has been practicing being a person all week, and he has been
> practicing *for you.*

Crisp pours two glasses. Tastes his. And for exactly one second he looks
*surprised.* "SOUR," he says. "AND YET." — that's Lawrence, peeking out.
Then the offer, built from the player's own Session Zero answer: whatever
they said they *love most about their dumb little town*, he offers it back,
perfected. Forever. And the reason, which should genuinely rattle them:

> "THE OTHER RESIDENTS WATCH TELEVISION. YOU — YOU WATCH THE STREET.
> WE ARE THE SAME, YOU AND I. WE HAVE BOTH BEEN WATCHING PINEBROOK
> FOR A VERY LONG TIME."

**Resolutions (all of them good):**
- **Polite refusal** → he nods, almost relieved, and says the saddest possible
  thing: "THEY SAID YOU WOULD SAY THAT. I AM... GLAD." No mechanical change;
  full respect established for the finale.
- **Rude refusal** → "THANK YOU FOR THE FEEDBACK." MARSH-2 skips a ladder rung.
  Worth it, maybe.
- **Acceptance (real or faked)** → the double-agent path: Suspicion −2, a drip
  of Vanguard intel each session — but allies notice the PC getting cozy with
  the HOA (each session undercover: one ally needs personal reassurance or
  drifts). And the bribe arrives: the thing they loved, perfected, is
  *creepier than any threat.* If they loved Duke's waffles, the waffles are
  suddenly, impossibly, exactly like their grandmother's. Ask the player how
  they know that. Watch their face.

## 4. The motif table (echoes make reveals feel inevitable)

Rule: each motif appears at least once per act. Plant casually, echo
sideways, pay off hard.

| Motif | Plant (Act 1) | Echo (Act 2) | Payoff (Act 3) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Porch lights** | The PC's flickers at 2:47 — opening image | Replaced houses leave theirs on all night, every night, all the same brightness | The ending's final image (§6) |
| **"Five more minutes"** | The PC's own snooze button, morning after — let them say it | The pajama-shuffling stranger murmurs it; a pod guest half-wakes with it | Herb's Choice — a whole marriage in three words |
| **"IT SURE IS WEATHER"** | GARY-2's catchphrase | Other clones inherit it — it's spreading through the network like a software tic | GLORP PRIME, mid-buffer, says it *sincerely*. In the Treaty epilogue humans say it to each other on purpose, and mean it |
| **The pending award** | The town sign: "(award pending since 1994)" | Bunny's display hospitalized the '94 judge; the Collective read the 32-page application | Treaty: Pinebrook finally wins — and votes to stay "pending" anyway. They like the hope better |
| **The B-flat hum** | "The fridge hums in B-flat" — a throwaway | Wendell: the tower hums *the same note as your fridge.* You've been hearing it your whole life | Marcus's tuba is the anti-note. The finale's free jazz is literally the town changing its key |
| **The mint on the pillow** | — | The pods: comfort, maximum | The bundt-cake twist; the RESERVED pod; Treaty gift baskets each contain one mint that nobody ever eats |
| **Gnomes rotating** | One appears (Suspicion 4) | They track like sunflowers; the Ninth Gnome's chalk arrows | The stinger: one gnome, somewhere, rotates three degrees. Cut to black |

## 5. Running gags (rule of three, then retire or promote)

- **Hodge's crayon drawings:** (1) accurate → (2) impossibly accurate → (3) of
  something that *hasn't happened yet.* Show the third one the night before
  Founders' Day: the water tower, fireworks spelling WOW, and a small figure
  labeled "THE ONE WHO'LL FIX IT." Hodge, shrugging: "Sometimes I draw ahead."
- **Gazette items about the PC**, politeness escalating into menace:
  "LOCAL RESIDENT ASKS ODD QUESTIONS" → "RESIDENT SEEN RUNNING; NO GAME
  SCHEDULED" → "AREA PERSON 'FINE,' SAYS EVERYONE ASKED."
- **The GARY sequence:** every destroyed or glitched GARY comes back one model
  worse. GARY-3 waves with the wrong hand. GARY-4's tie is painted on.
  GARY-5 introduces himself as "GARY HENDERSON, FELLOW HUSBAND OF THIS LAWN."
  (Cap it at 5. Rule of three, plus two, because it's that good.)
- **Duke's bell** develops grammar: one ding yes, two dings no, a furious
  paradiddle for "the pretzel test just failed."
- **"Look it up."** Doug Pramuk, constantly, about the unlookupable. Finale
  beat: someone finally looks one up — and Doug is right, once, about one (1)
  thing, forever. He frames the search results.

## 6. Lawrence (the villain is the saddest character — protect that)

**The arc:** 1987 — the loneliest kid at the reservoir *asks the light to
take him.* The Collective, horrified and honored (nobody had ever asked),
makes him the template and, eventually, the suit. The HOA presidency wasn't
strategy; it was the closest thing to living inside the broadcasts.

**Voice drift (playable in dialogue — the tell of his humanity):**
- **Act One:** pure institution. All caps, zero first person. "THE ASSOCIATION IS PLEASED."
- **Act Two:** exactly three "I" statements, all at the Offer, script them:
  *"I ASKED THEM FOR SOMETHING."* · *"I REMEMBER SOUR."* · *"I WATCHED TOO."*
- **Act Three:** at half HP, drop your volume and drop the caps: *"...we
  watched your broadcasts for forty years. we only wanted to live in one."*
  And if — only if — the player uses his name: a long pause, and then,
  quietly: *"...nobody has used that name since the reservoir."* (Charm DC
  drops to 8. The fight can end right here, mid-round, if the player has
  something true to say.)

**Lawrence's three endings:**
- **Treaty:** Deborah, of all people: "Motion to accept Lawrence Crisp,
  as-is. No amendments." It passes unanimously. He buffers for nine seconds —
  the longest buffer ever recorded in Pinebrook — and then weeps green
  glitter into the potluck coleslaw, and nobody minds.
- **Big Boom:** his suit washes up at the reservoir a week later. Empty,
  folded neatly, with a thank-you note. The Gazette runs it on page one.
  Arlene writes the headline gently.
- **Perfected:** he wins Friendliest Neighbor at the ceremony, and in the
  moment the applause is perfectly synchronized, he looks — one frame, blink
  and you miss it — completely alone. He got everything he asked the light
  for in 1987. Roll credits on his face.

## 7. Final images (each ending must rhyme with the opening)

The campaign opened on one porch light flickering at 2:47. It closes on
porch lights:

- **🕊 Treaty:** at 2:47 AM, every porch light on Marigold Lane blinks twice —
  on purpose, all together. It's the Collective, from their one model home,
  saying goodnight. The PC's light blinks back. (Ask the player if they
  installed the switch or if the house just... learned. Accept either.)
- **💥 Big Boom:** at 2:47 AM, nothing happens. No hum. No light. The PC sits
  on the porch anyway, out of habit, and finds — annoyingly, privately —
  that they miss it a little. Biscuit puts his head on their knee. "Yeah,"
  he says. "Me too."
- **🌱 Perfected:** at 2:47 AM, every light in Pinebrook comes on at once.
  Steady. Bright. None of them ever flicker again. That's the horror:
  nothing in this town will ever flicker again.

## 8. The seven rules of Pinebrook comedy (punch-up kit)

1. **Specificity is the joke.** Never "weird groceries" — always "fourteen
   gallons of pickle brine, purchased at 2:47 PM."
2. **Clones are funniest at 96% correct.** "EN-JOY YOUR MEAL" is nothing;
   "EN-JOY YOUR SOUP" — at a barbecue — is everything.
3. **Politeness is the horror AND the punchline.** The moment an alien is
   rude, the spell breaks. They never are. Even dissolving, they apologize
   for the mess.
4. **The town's denial outruns any evidence.** "Well — Gary always was formal."
5. **Biscuit speaks exactly when it's least convenient**, and he is always,
   technically, helping.
6. **Every scary thing gets one domestic detail.** The abduction beam yields
   to pedestrians. The invasion files compliments. The doomsday schedule has
   a line item for napkins.
7. **When a joke lands at the table, write it into canon immediately.**
   The player's laughter is the best co-writer you will ever get. Credit it
   nowhere. Use it everywhere.
