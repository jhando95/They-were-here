# Running It Loose — the Sandbox GM Guide
### For when the player ignores your beautiful plan (they will) · ⚠️ GM EYES ONLY

The act guides are a *shape*, not rails. This chapter is the toolkit that makes
any player choice work: go anywhere, talk to anyone, try anything — and the
mystery still holds together, because you prepped the **town**, not the plot.

---

## 1. The three invariants (everything else bends)

Only three facts are load-bearing. Protect these; improvise everything else freely:

1. **It always happens at 2:47.** Abductions, hums, deliveries, glitches.
2. **Salt burns them; genuine human weirdness makes them buffer.**
3. **The Collective is unfailingly polite.** They never do villainy that
   couldn't be mistaken for customer service.

If an improvised scene honors these three, it will feel like Pinebrook no
matter what else happens in it.

## 2. THE REVELATION WEB (the mystery as a net, not a line)

The player must eventually learn eight things. **None of them lives in only
one place** — every revelation has 3+ independent paths, so there is no scene
they can miss, no NPC they can alienate, no door they can burn down that
breaks the story. This web is also in the app: **Quests tab → THE TRAIL (GM)**
— check revelations off as they land and glance at the remaining paths
mid-session.

| # | Revelation | Any of these paths delivers it |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Gary was replaced — and he's not the only one** | any clone-tell field test · Chet's Follicle File · Big Ang's snack spreadsheet · Hal Butterfield's mow-log · Dakota's Brine List |
| R2 | **It happens at 2:47 AM, on a schedule** | Wendell's scanner log · Opal's split-log book · Priya's doorbell-reboot data · Dez's VHS bowling tape · staying up once |
| R3 | **The HOA is the front; Crisp runs it** | Tilly's DECLINED letter · Maureen's permit trail · Carol Ann's verbatim minutes · the block-party keycard · the Gazette's 1987 photo caption |
| R4 | **The real neighbors are alive, beneath FreshMart** | Biscuit's nose · Gen-Gen's MALL BELOW map · buffering KYLE at the freight elevator · Fran's deli-schedule gap · Duke's bricked wall |
| R5 | **The lawns are an antenna; the tower is the beacon** | Priya's drone photo · Gus's lawn diary · Walt Grubb's growth spreadsheets · climbing the tower · Doug Wexler's citation ledger |
| R6 | **Founders' Day is the Bloom — fireworks + beacon = fleet** | the Undermart schematic wall · the fireworks invoice (via Maureen OR Carol's receipt shoebox) · a BEV-2 gossip trade · Arlene's spiked follow-up |
| R7 | **Salt hurts them; weirdness jams them** | the spore-grass sample hissing · Nana Ruthie's doorstep · Duke's pretzel test · Terry Loomis's camcorder tape · any accidental buffer moment |
| R8 | **The pods open to the Overseer's handshake** | the hand-shaped pod recesses · a KYLE or TODD-2 interrogation · a BEV-2 trade · Gil Herrera's 1989 memory of what burned out of him |

**The magnetic-clue rule:** clues are attached to the *revelation*, not the
location. If the player never visits the Gazette, the 1987 story can surface
as Nana Ruthie's Polaroid, Coral's microfiche, or a Truther's binder. Move the
clue to wherever the player actually is; keep the *content* identical.

**The three-strike delivery:** if a revelation has been available three times
and hasn't landed, hand it over for free in the next scene — dramatically,
via an NPC (Biscuit blurting it mid-nap is always legal). Mysteries die from
clue starvation, never from generosity.

## 3. When they go somewhere you didn't prep

Any unlisted place in Pinebrook — the bowling alley, the DMV, a random
backyard — is three ingredients:

1. **A person with a bit** — grab anyone from the census (Folks tab, hit
   search), or build one in five seconds: *a job + one absurd specific detail
   + what they want right now.*
2. **One thing slightly wrong** — pick from: a new gnome · brine where brine
   shouldn't be · plastic flowers · a too-perfect lawn · unison laughter two
   seconds late · a FRSHMRT LOGISTICS box.
3. **One thread back to the web** — the wrong thing connects to whichever
   revelation the player is *closest to not knowing.* (Check THE TRAIL.)

That's it. Person, wrongness, thread. Every location in this campaign was
built with that formula; yours will match seamlessly.

**When they talk to someone you didn't prep:** roll a d12 on the Clone Tells
table — even = they're human (give them a want), odd = give them ONE tell and
let the player sweat about whether it means anything. (Sometimes a
never-blinking man is just a man. That ambiguity is free suspense.)

## 4. When they do something you didn't expect

- **"Yes, and the town notices."** Say yes to the plan — then touch a clock.
  Loud/public → Suspicion +1. Weird/unhinged-looking → Reputation +1.
  Quiet and clever → a clue, free. The clocks make ANY choice meaningful
  without you needing to invent consequences from scratch.
- **If their plan is better than yours, it works.** Never punish better
  writing. Charge a *cost* instead — pick one: it takes until 2:47 · it needs
  an ally's help (spotlight!) · it works but leaves evidence with their name
  on it.
- **Steal their headcanon.** When the player theorizes something better than
  your truth ("wait — is the DINER built on the tunnel?"), it was always
  true. Nod slowly. Write it down. They will tell everyone you planned it.
- **Skipping ahead is allowed.** If they beeline to the water tower in
  session one: let them! They find the locked hatch (needs Crisp's keycard
  — now the block party matters), the spore-grass, and a Suspicion +2 tail.
  Early access to a location is never early access to the *finale* — the
  Bloom still needs Founders' Day, and the pods still need the handshake.
  The two gates (keycard, handshake) are the only doors in the campaign;
  everything else is open world.
- **If they kill something you needed:** nobody human dies in Pinebrook, and
  clones are *replaceable by definition* — a destroyed GARY-2 is replaced by
  GARY-3, who is 4% worse at pretending ("HELLO, FELLOW... HUMAN? YES.").
  Escalation, not derailment.

## 5. The "NOW WHAT?" oracle (d12 — also the 🧭 button in the app)

When the player stalls, or you blank, or the table needs a spark — roll.
Every entry pushes toward the web without railroading:

1. Biscuit arrives, eats something evidence-adjacent, and announces — between
   licks — exactly where he found it.
2. The phone rings: BEV-2, "RECIPROCATING." She leaks one real Vanguard fact
   and demands gossip back. She can hear you deciding whether to lie.
3. A MISSED DELIVERY slip flutters on the door. It's dated *tomorrow*,
   2:47 AM. The addressee is a neighbor the player likes.
4. Priya's drone crash-lands at their feet with eleven seconds of footage
   somebody clearly shot down to suppress.
5. The MARSH-2 cruiser rolls past at exactly two under the limit.
   Third time today. She waves. She's practicing waving.
6. Mrs. Pemberton needs her casserole back. It's "restless." It is pointing
   at something, the way a compass points.
7. A Tuesday Truther bursts in: their dumbest theory just accidentally
   produced a completely real document.
8. The gnome count on this street has changed since breakfast.
9. Wendell over the walkie, whisper-screaming: the Lawn Frequency just spiked
   — *early*. It has never once been early.
10. A stranger in pajamas shuffles past, half-awake, murmuring "five more
    minutes." Nobody in town recognizes them. The pods don't lose count.
    Do they?
11. Deborah cites a nearby clone for a violation — and its response is a
    sound no polite thing should make.
12. It is suddenly 2:46 AM. Wherever they are. Whatever they were doing.
    Something starts to hum.

## 6. The five-minute session prep sheet

Before each session, answer six questions (pencil, index card, done):

1. **Where did we leave them, and what's the obvious next thing?** (Have a
   cold-open ready for it — and be delighted when they don't do it.)
2. **Which revelations are still unchecked on THE TRAIL?** Pick the two
   nearest, and note one path to each from where the player currently is.
3. **What's the Opposition move this session?** (One rung of MARSH-2's
   ladder, or one Crisp countermeasure. Just one.)
4. **Which clocks move if the player does nothing?** (Usually: Reputation +1,
   courtesy of MARSH-2.)
5. **Which twist do I plant tonight?** (Arm it in the app. Fire nothing you
   haven't planted.)
6. **Which two NPCs do I want to say out loud?** (Pick from the census for
   joy, not plot. Plot will find them.)

Then play the recap card and follow the player. That's the whole job — the
web catches whatever they do.

## 7. The GM's emergency card (tape inside your screen)

- Stuck? → **NOW WHAT? d12.**
- They went somewhere weird? → **person + wrongness + thread.**
- They learned nothing this scene? → **magnetic clue: move one to them.**
- Their theory is cooler than mine? → **it was always true.**
- Their plan is airtight? → **it works; charge a cost.**
- I don't know what this NPC knows? → **THE TRAIL: they know one path to the
  revelation the player is closest to missing.**
- Everything is chaos? → **It is suddenly 2:46 AM.**
